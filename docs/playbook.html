Built to be trusted

  • Server-side pricing — guests can't change prices from their phone.
  • Rules enforced — required options, sold-out items and serving hours.
  • Secure QR codes — random codes, replaceable in one click if abused.
  • Anti-spam — limits on orders and waiter calls per table.
  • Separate data per restaurant; staff roles with limited access.
  • Encrypted passwords, protected forms, safe photo uploads.
  • Automatic daily backups, exact money maths, tax & service charge.
  • Tested — 34 automated tests + full browser tests.

Section 6

Going live: hosting & setup

06

Important: on your PC, the QR codes only work for guests on the same Wi-Fi. To sell it as a service, TableTap must be online so any guest on 4G/5G can order.

Buy a domain

e.g. tabletap-yourcity.com (≈ $10–15/year). Short and easy to type.

Rent a small server (VPS)

2 GB RAM is plenty for your first 50–100 restaurants (≈ $5–12/month at Hetzner, DigitalOcean, Contabo, OVH…). Choose a data centre close to your customers.

Install TableTap + HTTPS

Copy the folder, pip install -r requirements.txt, run behind Caddy or nginx for free HTTPS certificates. Set TABLETAP_PUBLIC_URL, TABLETAP_TRUST_PROXY=1, TABLETAP_SECURE_COOKIES=1. Run it as a service so it restarts automatically.

Point the domain to the server

DNS A-record → server IP. Test from a phone on 4G.

Backups off the server

Daily backups are created automatically; also copy them weekly to another place (your PC or cloud storage).

Create your demo restaurant

Beautiful photos, 20+ dishes, a printed tent card with its QR code — this is your sales weapon.

Alternative for a single restaurantRun TableTap on the restaurant's PC and expose it with a named Cloudflare Tunnel on your domain. Don't use temporary tunnel URLs — they change and break printed QR codes.

The three numbers that decide if you win

CAC

Cost to win one client = (ads + your time + gifts) ÷ new clients. Target: less than 3 months of subscription.

Churn

% of clients cancelling each month. Target: under 3%. Good onboarding + support keep it low.

LTV

Lifetime value = monthly price ÷ churn. $39 ÷ 3% ≈ $1,300 per client. You can afford to spend to win clients.

Simple ruleIf a client is worth ~$1,300 over their lifetime, spending $50–150 in ads and time to win them is a great deal. Don't be afraid to invest in acquisition once your trial-to-paid rate is proven.
Section 11

Sales system (the pipeline)

11

Selling is a machine with stages. Track every restaurant in one of these stages (a spreadsheet is fine at the start).

Find — build a list of 300 restaurants in your city

Use Lead Finder by category (restaurant, café, hotel, bar) and area. Export name, phone, email, website, address, rating.

Qualify — score each lead A / B / C

A = busy, 4+ rating, many reviews, paper/PDF menu or tourist area. B = decent. C = tiny or closing. Run Website Inspector on their site: no menu page or slow site = A. Start with A leads only.

Personalise — prepare a mini-demo for A leads

Create their restaurant in your platform with their name, colour, logo and 5–8 real dishes from their Instagram/Google photos. Print one tent card with its QR. This is the #1 closing trick.

Contact — multi-channel sequence (Section 12)

Day 1 email (MailBlaster) → Day 3 WhatsApp → Day 5 walk-in with the printed QR → Day 9 follow-up email → Day 14 last message.

Demo — 10 minutes, on the owner's phone

Let them scan and order. The kitchen screen beeps on your tablet. Show the dashboard. Ask the ROI questions (Section 8).

Trial — 14 days, fully set up by you

Full menu, QR stands on tables, 15-minute staff training. Visit/call on day 2 and day 7.

Close — day 12–14

Show their numbers (orders through TableTap, average bill, best sellers). Offer yearly (2 months free) first, then monthly. Ask for a video testimonial and 2 referrals.

Onboarding a new restaurant

16

A perfect first week = a client for years. Target: from "yes" to live in 48 hours.

Collect (day 0)

Menu (photo/PDF — convert with File Studio), logo, dish photos, prices, tax rules, table list/zones, Wi-Fi name, staff names & roles.

Build (day 1)

Platform → New restaurant (plan + paid-until date + manager login). Settings: logo, cover, colour, currency, tax, language, time zone. Categories with serving hours; items with photos, options and extras; mark popular items.

Tables & QR (day 1)

Add tables in bulk with zones. Print table cards or tent cards; put them in acrylic stands. Test-scan every single QR code.

Kitchen (day 2)

Tablet or TV with the kitchen account, full screen, sound enabled, "keep awake". Print test ticket if they use a printer.

Train (day 2, 15 minutes)

Waiters: live orders, calls, settle a table, mark paid. Kitchen: start/ready, 86 switch. Manager: change a price, mark sold-out, dashboard.

Go live + stay close

Be there for the first service if possible. Call on day 2 and day 7. Fix small things fast — it builds trust.

Section 20

Legal & professional basics

20

General guidance, not legal advice — check the rules in your country with a local accountant or lawyer.

  • Register a business (sole trader / auto-entrepreneur / company) so you can invoice, open a business bank account, and get WhatsApp Business verification.
  • Simple contract / terms of service: plan, price, billing date, 7-day grace then suspension, cancellation notice, who owns the menu content, support hours, uptime "best effort".
  • Privacy policy: you store guest names, notes and orders on behalf of the restaurant. Explain what, why, how long, and how to delete. Respect GDPR if you have EU guests/clients.
  • Invoices for every payment; keep records for taxes.
  • Email marketing: business addresses only, honest subject lines, your identity, unsubscribe link, stop when asked. (MailBlaster handles unsubscribe + suppression.)
  • Security habits: strong platform password, backups copied off the server weekly, server updates monthly.
  • Allergen info: remind restaurants they are responsible for the allergen data they enter.
